What Mazda Does The bookkeeping agent, and how she keeps us on the ROL Finance Plan

Self-hosted Letta deployment · Agent agent-6b536cf4-… · Written 2026-08-04
Mazda is the agent who turns paper into bookkeeping. Every scanned receipt and every bank statement that comes through the scanner is handed to her, and she is responsible for reading it, matching it to a vendor, categorizing it, checking it is not already recorded, filing the image somewhere permanent, and storing the result in the finance database — with a written trace of what she did. She is the reason the 2025 numbers have documents behind them instead of memory behind them.

1. Who she is

Mazda is a persistent Letta agent, not a chat session. She keeps her own memory between runs, so what she learns about a vendor or a statement layout in March is still with her in August. She orchestrates — her own model is small and cheap, and she hands the heavy reading and coding work to Claude through the SDK. That keeps her running cost low enough to process hundreds of documents.

She does not work alone. A second agent, the Trainer, is launched automatically alongside every single scan she processes. The Trainer watches her run, checks her work against the intake contract, and coaches her by message when she gets something wrong. Her work is graded, every time, without anyone having to watch.

2. Her responsibilities, step by step

Every document she touches goes through the same nine steps. The dashboard shows these live as she works, so you can see exactly where a scan is.

  1. Load learned wrapper. Pick up the current version of her own instructions — including any corrections the Trainer taught her since the last run.
  2. Classify and parse. Decide what the document is (bank statement, receipt, printed statement photo) and pull out dates, amounts, descriptions and line items. Cheapest reliable tool first; a model only when the deterministic parser cannot do it.
  3. Check vendor and duplicates. Collapse merchant name variants to one canonical vendor, then check whether this charge is already in the books.
  4. Categorize. Look the vendor up in the chart of accounts. Only ask a model when there is no reliable lookup.
  5. Store. Write the expense rows to the finance database and attach the supporting document.
  6. Record trace. Write down what she actually did, with evidence.
  7. Judge trace. Score her own run against the contract.
  8. Propose improvement. If she failed, propose a fix to her own instructions rather than repeating the mistake next time.
  9. Notify dashboard. Publish the result so it appears in the reports and the scanner tabs.

Evidence: four slots, and they accumulate

An expense can carry up to four independent supporting documents, and a new one never replaces an old one:

SlotWhat it is
ReceiptThe scanned till receipt or invoice.
Source documentThe statement the bank itself issued and we downloaded.
Mom's ledgerThe handwritten ledger page.
Scanned statementA photograph of a printed statement, archived by year.

Everything is filed to a permanent path with a findable name, and the database row points at that permanent copy — never at the temporary scanner folder. The rule behind this is simple: if you have to go back to a document a year from now, it has to still be where the row says it is.

3. How this carries out the ROL Finance Plan

The plan sets out what a nonprofit's finances have to be able to do. Mazda is the part that makes several of those requirements actually happen day to day rather than existing on paper.

Plan requirementWhat Mazda contributes
Expense management — process and track all expenses with proper documentation Every expense she stores is created from a document, so the documentation is never a separate chore that falls behind.
Audit trail — a clear record of every transaction Each run writes a trace: what was read, what was decided, what evidence supported it. The Trainer's report sits alongside it as an independent check.
Receipts / invoices mandatory The four evidence slots, plus the dashboard's corner markers, make it visible at a glance which rows still have nothing behind them.
Bank reconciliation Statement totals are verified against the rows she stored; a statement that does not add up is flagged rather than filed.
Chart of accounts Categories live in the database behind a single interface, so the accounts structure can be corrected in one place and every report follows.
Reporting Her output feeds the verified-transaction reports the dashboard publishes, which are what a finance committee or an accountant would actually read.
Form 990 readiness Categorized, documented, deduplicated rows are exactly the raw material an annual filing needs. The crosswalk from our categories to IRS lines is drafted but not yet blessed by an accountant.

4. The rules she is held to

Never invent anything

No transaction, total, or database match may be fabricated. If a parse is incomplete, it is reported incomplete.

Ambiguous beats wrong

When two existing rows could both be the same charge, she reports ambiguous instead of picking one. Same for the medical expenses that cannot be attributed to Robert or Rosemary — they go to a visible unassigned bucket, never a guess.

Money movement is not spending

Transfers between accounts and credit-card payoffs sit in the same table as real expenses but must not be counted as expenses — at one point they inflated the raw total by roughly half. They are classified by payee, never by keyword, because paying a real vendor through online bill pay is a real expense. Rows are never deleted, only reclassified, so the totals can still be tied back to the statements.

One bad row does not sink the statement

A single unparseable line is excluded on its own; the rest of the statement still files. A statement with a genuinely unsafe row goes to a review folder instead.

She never blocks

If the Trainer or any supporting service fails, intake still completes. The safety net is not allowed to become the failure.

The pipeline works and the bulk of 2025 is in. What remains is mostly accuracy and presentation, not new machinery.

  • Re-run the money-movement pass after the last import. It is idempotent and must be run after every statement import, or transfers and card payoffs will count as spending in the final numbers.
  • Teach the grand total to skip excluded buckets. The spreadsheet still sums every reporting bucket, so Money Movement shows up as a line inside the total instead of being left out of it. Small fix, visible effect on the headline number.
  • Fix the known-wrong category rollups. Four are documented and deliberately left in place pending a dry-run approval — most importantly around thirty-two of Rosemary's medical expenses currently defaulting to Robert.
  • Decide the Personal question. Whether category 3 (Personal) is excluded from the nonprofit totals is still open, and it changes the reported figure.
  • Resolve the eight unknown card rows. Roughly $1,500 across two card numbers nobody recognises, left counting as spending until Mom can confirm what they were. These should not be guessed.
  • Get an accountant's sign-off on the category→IRS mapping. No filing conclusion is final without the church's accountant reviewing the crosswalk.
